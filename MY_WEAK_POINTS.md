### 1. "Survey" contribution ≠ a questionnaire survey
- **What tripped me:** I kept thinking "survey" = sending a questionnaire to users. Got it backwards three times.
- **The fix:** In the 7 contribution types, **"Survey" = LITERATURE REVIEW** — reading & synthesizing *already-published papers* to map trends/gaps. **No participants, no new data.**
  - A questionnaire/interview/experiment with real people = **EMPIRICAL** (always).
- **Memory hook:** 📚 **Library = Survey** (review papers) · 🔬 **Lab = Empirical** (collect data from people). Rename "survey" → **"literature review"** in my head.
- **The test to apply:** *Does the paper study PEOPLE/SYSTEMS, or study OTHER PAPERS?* People → empirical. Other papers → survey.

## 🟠 PRIORITY 2 — Needed correcting, core exam material

### 2. IV vs DV (do NOT confuse with statistical correlation)
- **What tripped me:** I described them as "variables that depend on each other" (that's correlation, not experiment design).
- **The fix:**
  - **IV (Independent Variable) = what the researcher CHANGES** = the *cause* = the design being tested. Its values = **levels**.
  - **DV (Dependent Variable) = what you MEASURE** = the *effect* (time, errors, satisfaction).
- **Memory hook:** `IV (I change it) → causes → DV (I measure it). The effect DEPENDS on the cause.`
- **Example:** Voice vs touch keyboard → **IV = keyboard type** (levels: voice/touch); **DV = speed & errors**.
- ⚠️ Participant traits (age, vision) are NOT the IV/DV — they're characteristics you control/record.

### 3. Reuse — recycling INPUT vs OUTPUT (and don't confuse with polymorphism)
- **What tripped me:** First I described the Share button (that's **polymorphism**, not reuse). Then mixed up input/output.
- **The fix:**
  - **Reuse = recycling PAST interactions to speed up FUTURE actions** (it's about *time/history*, not object types).
  - **Recycling INPUT** = app hands back **choices I entered** before (recent searches, "reorder my last meal", Maps "swap" origin/destination).
  - **Recycling OUTPUT** = I **save a produced result as a reusable object** (save a route as a shortcut, save a colour as a swatch, reuse a ChatGPT answer).
- **Memory hook:**
  - **Polymorphism** = *one command, many object TYPES* (Share on song/artist/playlist).
  - **Reuse** = *recycle my past input/output to save effort* (past → future).
  - Within reuse: *did the app remember what I typed/picked?* → **input**. *Did I save a result as a new reusable thing?* → **output**.

### 4. Privacy paradox = SAYS vs DOES
- **What tripped me:** I described "expectations vs reality of how data is handled." Wrong — that's about awareness.
- **The fix:** Privacy paradox = the contradiction **inside the user**: they **SAY** they care about privacy (stated concern) but **DO** the opposite (don't read policies, click "Accept all", use creepy apps anyway).
- **Memory hook:** **"Concerned in words, careless in actions."** = attitudinal (what they say) vs behavioral (what they do).
- **Linked concept:** *learned helplessness / privacy fatigue* explains WHY — after repeated invasions with no recourse, people give up *even when given tools to defend themselves*.

### 5. Social presence vs Selective self-presentation
- **What tripped me:** Muddled them together and mixed in modalities.
- **The fix:**
  - **Social presence / co-presence** = the **sense of "being together"** — how much a feature makes the other person feel *present/here with me*. (Typing indicator, read receipts → increase it.)
  - **Selective self-presentation** = using a medium's **limitations to control how others see me** (editing my text before sending = polished image).
- **The tension (memorize — it's the Typing Indicators paper's whole point):**
  > Richer/revealing features (live typing) → **↑ social presence** but **↓ selective self-presentation.** You feel more together, but lose control over your self-image.

### 6. Walled garden — locks in CONTACTS *and* FUNCTIONALITY
- **What tripped me:** Couldn't cleanly say what it isolates; answered "both" when it was one specific thing.
- **The fix:**
  - **Walled garden** = the overall design (app/device walled off). It **locks in TWO things:**
    1. **Contacts (people)** — can't reach another app's users (can't iMessage an Android friend).
    2. **Functionality (features)** — can't use another app's features here (can't AirDrop to Windows; can't use WhatsApp stickers in Messenger).
- **The test:** *Is the blocked thing a PERSON I want to reach (→ contacts) or a FEATURE I want to use (→ functionality)?*
- **Result it causes:** **Expression breakdown** = conversation flow breaks when I miss a feature from another app.

## 🟡 PRIORITY 3 — Forgot terms / details (knew the concept once reminded)

### 7. Don't confuse the THREE LISTS
I mixed these up when asked for "mobile challenges." Keep them separate:
| List | Items |
|---|---|
| **Mobile design challenges** | reduced screen real estate · occlusion / fat finger · variable contexts · limited input expressiveness |
| **Contexts of use** | physical · temporal · task · social · technical |
| **Interaction qualities** | discoverability · efficiency · expressiveness |

### 8. Three waves of HCI — I forget WAVE 2
- **1st = performance** (efficiency, errors; user = machine/processor)
- **2nd = work / social-organizational context** (groups, workplaces) ← *the one I forget*
- **3rd = experience, meaning, wellbeing, everyday life**
- **Hook:** **Machine → Work → Life** (Lab → Office → Home).

### 9. Context type misclassifications
- "Eating / mouth full" = **physical** (body) or **task** — NOT temporal.
- "Phone damaged / bad mic" = **technical** — NOT physical.
- **Temporal** = *time* things (in a hurry, time of day, before/after, real-time vs async).
- **Physical** = environment + my body (lighting, weather, location, movement, vision/hearing).

### 10. Expressiveness framing
- It's about the **USER'S range of input possibilities** (many ways to express intent) — NOT "the system expressing itself."
- Good example: send the same feeling as a **crying video OR a crying emoji** = wide input range.

### 11. Within/Between + Order effects (knew concept, forgot terms)
- **Within-subjects** = everyone does ALL conditions → risk of **order effects** (learning/fatigue from sequence) → fix with **counterbalancing** (vary the order across people).
- **Between-subjects** = each group does ONE condition (one **level** of the IV) → avoids order effects but needs more people.

### 12. Modality — the definition
- **A structured communication channel between human and technology, based on a human expressive or perceptual capability.** = a way to send/receive info using a human sense or action.
- Input (I express): touch, voice, gesture, gaze. Output (I perceive): visual, auditory, haptic.
- ⚠️ Modality ≠ sensing tech: *microphone* = sensor; *voice* = modality.

## ⚪ GAPS — I skipped these, need to actually learn them

### 13. p-value (quant analysis)
- **Plain English:** *"If there were really NO effect (H₀ true), how likely is a result this big just by chance?"* Small p (< .05) = rare by chance → **reject H₀** → effect probably real.
- ⚠️ Does NOT prove the effect and says nothing about its **size** → always also report **effect size** (Cohen's d, η², r).

### 14. Media Richness Theory (had "video" but not the claim)
- **Claim:** **richer media** (video, face-to-face — many cues) are **better for COMPLEX/emotional messages**; **lean media** (text) are fine for **simple** ones. It's about *matching medium richness to message complexity*.
- Hook: emotional/complex → **rich** (video call); "meeting at 3pm" → **lean** (text).
